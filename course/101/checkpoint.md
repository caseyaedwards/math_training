# Level 101 Checkpoint

Use this self-test after you finish all ten lessons. Work on paper, without a calculator, and give yourself about 60 minutes. Answer every question before you open any answer. Each answer names the lesson to revisit if you miss it.

## Questions

### Place Value and Operations

1. Write `40,506` in expanded form, and give the value of the digit `5`.
2. Round `8,451` to the nearest hundred.
3. Draw the area model for `27 × 16` and compute the product.
4. Compute `250 ÷ 8` as a quotient with a remainder, and write the check `250 = 8 × q + r`.

<details>
<summary>Answers 1 to 4</summary>

1. `4 × 10,000 + 0 × 1,000 + 5 × 100 + 0 × 10 + 6 × 1`. The `5` is worth `500`. [Lesson 1](01-place-value-and-the-number-line/README.md).
2. `8,500`: the tens digit is `5`, which rounds up. [Lesson 1](01-place-value-and-the-number-line/README.md).
3. `432`: `20 × 10 = 200`, `7 × 10 = 70`, `20 × 6 = 120`, `7 × 6 = 42`, and `200 + 70 + 120 + 42 = 432`. [Lesson 2](02-the-four-operations/README.md).
4. `31` remainder `2`: `250 = 8 × 31 + 2`. [Lesson 2](02-the-four-operations/README.md).

</details>

### Negative Numbers

5. Compute `−7 − (−12)`.
6. Compute `(−4) × (−5) ÷ (−10)`.
7. Two thermometers read `−8` degrees and `5` degrees. How far apart are the readings?

<details>
<summary>Answers 5 to 7</summary>

5. `5`: subtracting `−12` adds `12`. [Lesson 3](03-negative-numbers/README.md).
6. `−2`: `(−4) × (−5) = 20`, then `20 ÷ (−10) = −2`. [Lesson 3](03-negative-numbers/README.md).
7. `13` degrees: `5 − (−8) = 13`. [Lesson 3](03-negative-numbers/README.md).

</details>

### Factors and Primes

8. Find the prime factorization of `252`.
9. Find $\gcd(36, 90)$ and $\operatorname{lcm}(36, 90)$.
10. Is `119` prime? Explain in one line.

<details>
<summary>Answers 8 to 10</summary>

8. $252 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7$. [Lesson 4](04-factors-multiples-and-primes/README.md).
9. GCD `18` and LCM `180`: $36 = 2^2 \times 3^2$ and $90 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 5$. Check: `18 × 180 = 3,240 = 36 × 90`. [Lesson 4](04-factors-multiples-and-primes/README.md).
10. No: `119 = 7 × 17`. [Lesson 4](04-factors-multiples-and-primes/README.md).

</details>

### Fractions

11. Simplify `42/56`, then decide which is larger: `42/56` or `5/7`.
12. Compute $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4}$.
13. Compute $1\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{9}$.
14. Compute $\frac{9}{10} \div \frac{3}{5}$, and say what the answer means in words.

<details>
<summary>Answers 11 to 14</summary>

11. `3/4`, which is larger than `5/7`: over `28`, `21/28 > 20/28`. [Lesson 5](05-fractions-meaning-and-equivalence/README.md).
12. `7/12`: `10/12 − 3/12`. [Lesson 6](06-fraction-operations/README.md).
13. `2/3`: `3/2 × 4/9 = 12/18`. [Lesson 6](06-fraction-operations/README.md).
14. `3/2`, which is `1 1/2`: `9/10 × 5/3 = 45/30`. Three fifths fits into nine tenths one and a half times. [Lesson 6](06-fraction-operations/README.md).

</details>

### Decimals, Percents and Ratios

15. Write `5/8` as a decimal and a percent. Then compute `2.4 × 0.15`.
16. A service had `1,250` errors last week and `1,000` this week. What is the percent change?
17. Concrete mix uses cement, sand and gravel in the ratio `1 : 2 : 3`. How much sand goes into `90 kg` of mix?
18. A job processes `2,400` records in `8` minutes. At the same rate, how long do `9,000` records take?

<details>
<summary>Answers 15 to 18</summary>

15. `0.625` and `62.5%`. Then `2.4 × 0.15 = 0.36`: `24 × 15 = 360`, with three decimal places. [Lesson 7](07-decimals-and-percents/README.md).
16. `−20%`: `(1,000 − 1,250) / 1,250 = −250/1,250 = −0.2`. [Lesson 7](07-decimals-and-percents/README.md).
17. `30 kg`: `6` parts of `15 kg`, sand gets `2` parts. [Lesson 8](08-ratios-proportions-and-rates/README.md).
18. `30` minutes: the unit rate is `300` records per minute, and `9,000 ÷ 300 = 30`. [Lesson 8](08-ratios-proportions-and-rates/README.md).

</details>

### Exponents, Order of Operations and Expressions

19. Evaluate `48 ÷ (2 + 6) × 3 − 2³`.
20. Simplify `5(2x − 3) − 4x + 9`, then evaluate the result at `x = −2`.

<details>
<summary>Answers 19 to 20</summary>

19. `10`: `48 ÷ 8 = 6`, `6 × 3 = 18`, `2³ = 8`, `18 − 8 = 10`. [Lesson 9](09-exponents-roots-and-order-of-operations/README.md).
20. `6x − 6`, which is `−18` at `x = −2`: `10x − 15 − 4x + 9 = 6x − 6`, and `6(−2) − 6 = −18`. [Lesson 10](10-variables-and-expressions/README.md).

</details>

## Ready for 201 If

Score one point per fully correct question. Then check the rubric below. The score alone does not decide readiness. The three skill checks matter as much.

| Score | Meaning | Next Step |
|---|---|---|
| 18 to 20 | Solid foundation | Move on to [201 Algebra I and Geometry](../201/README.md). |
| 14 to 17 | Mostly ready | Redo the lessons named in your missed answers, then retake those questions. |
| Below 14 | Gaps remain | Revisit the weakest unit first, using the [prerequisite map](README.md#prerequisite-map). Retake the full checkpoint in a week. |

You are ready for 201 if all of the following hold:

* **Score:** 18 or more out of 20.
* **No weak unit:** you missed at most one question in any single unit heading above.
* **Fractions and negatives are automatic:** questions 5, 6, 12, 13 and 14 felt routine, not like puzzles. Algebra in 201 uses these steps on every line.
* **You can draw it:** you can sketch, from memory, the area model for `27 × 16`, the number-line move for `−7 − (−12)`, and the unit-square picture for a fraction product.
* **You can explain it:** you can say out loud, in a sentence each, why subtracting a negative adds, why flip-and-multiply works, and why `3(x + 2)` equals `3x + 6`.

Record your score and the date in the Obsidian note for 101 Foundations, under the Checkpoint section.
