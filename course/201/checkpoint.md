# Course 201 Checkpoint

**Level:** 201 · **Questions:** 20 · **Time:** 90 minutes, closed book, calculator allowed for the last step only

Take this cold before starting 201 to see what you can skip, or after Lesson 12 to see whether you are ready for [Course 301](../301/README.md).
Write every step. A right answer with no steps scores half. That is how real exams work, and how real postmortems work too.
Each question names the lesson to revisit if you miss it.

## Questions

### Equations and Inequalities

1. Solve $4(x - 2) = 2x + 6$. ([Lesson 01](01-solving-linear-equations/README.md))
2. Solve $\dfrac{x + 3}{2} = \dfrac{2x - 1}{3}$ and check your answer. ([Lesson 01](01-solving-linear-equations/README.md))
3. Solve $-3x + 2 \ge 11$. Write the answer in interval notation and describe its number-line picture. ([Lesson 02](02-inequalities/README.md))

### Functions and Lines

4. $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$. Find $f(-3)$. Then decide whether $\{(2, 5), (3, 5), (2, 7)\}$ is a function, and why. ([Lesson 03](03-coordinate-plane-and-functions/README.md))
5. Find the equation of the line through $(-1, 4)$ and $(3, -4)$ in the form $y = mx + b$. ([Lesson 04](04-slope-and-lines/README.md))
6. Find the line perpendicular to $y = 3x - 1$ that passes through $(3, 1)$. ([Lesson 04](04-slope-and-lines/README.md))

### Systems

7. Solve $3x + 2y = 7$ and $x - 2y = 5$. ([Lesson 05](05-systems-of-equations/README.md))
8. A fleet has 40 virtual machines. Small ones cost 3 dollars per hour, large ones 8 dollars per hour. The fleet costs 195 dollars per hour. How many of each? ([Lesson 05](05-systems-of-equations/README.md))

### Exponents and Quadratics

9. Simplify $\dfrac{(3x^2 y)^3}{9x^4 y}$. ([Lesson 06](06-exponents-and-scientific-notation/README.md))
10. Compute $(8 \times 10^{-3})(5 \times 10^{7})$ in scientific notation. ([Lesson 06](06-exponents-and-scientific-notation/README.md))
11. Factor $x^2 - 3x - 28$, then solve $x^2 - 3x - 28 = 0$. ([Lesson 07](07-factoring-and-quadratics/README.md))
12. Solve $2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0$ with the quadratic formula. State the discriminant. ([Lesson 07](07-factoring-and-quadratics/README.md))

### Angles and Triangles

13. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. A pair of alternate interior angles measure $3x + 15$ and $5x - 25$ degrees. Find $x$ and both angles. ([Lesson 08](08-points-lines-and-angles/README.md))
14. The angles of a triangle are $x$, $2x$, and $3x$. Find them and classify the triangle by its angles. ([Lesson 09](09-triangles-congruence-and-similarity/README.md))
15. A 1.5 m stick casts a 2 m shadow. At the same moment a flagpole casts a 14 m shadow. How tall is the flagpole? Name the fact that justifies your method. ([Lesson 09](09-triangles-congruence-and-similarity/README.md))
16. Explain in two or three sentences, with a sketch, why $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ for a right triangle. ([Lesson 10](10-pythagorean-theorem/README.md))
17. Find the distance from $(-3, 2)$ to $(5, -4)$. ([Lesson 10](10-pythagorean-theorem/README.md))

### Area and Volume

18. A circle has area $49\pi$. Find its circumference, exact and to two decimals. ([Lesson 11](11-area-perimeter-and-circles/README.md))
19. A 10 by 10 square has a quarter circle of radius 10 cut from it, centered at one corner. Find the area that remains, exact and to two decimals. ([Lesson 11](11-area-perimeter-and-circles/README.md))
20. A cylinder has radius 4 and height 9. Find its volume and surface area, exact. Then find the volume of a cone with the same base and height. ([Lesson 12](12-volume-and-surface-area/README.md))

## Answers

<details>
<summary>Answers 1 to 5</summary>

1. $x = 7$.

   ```txt
   4x - 8 = 2x + 6     distribute
   2x - 8 = 6          subtract 2x
   2x     = 14         add 8
    x     = 7          divide by 2
   ```

2. $x = 11$. Multiply both sides by 6: $3(x + 3) = 2(2x - 1)$, which gives $3x + 9 = 4x - 2$ and $x = 11$. Check: $\tfrac{14}{2} = 7$ and $\tfrac{21}{3} = 7$.
3. $x \le -3$, interval $(-\infty, -3]$: a closed circle at $-3$ with a ray to the left. Subtract 2 to get $-3x \ge 9$, then divide by $-3$ and flip.
4. $f(-3) = 9 + 6 = 15$. Not a function: the input 2 has two outputs, 5 and 7.
5. $y = -2x + 2$. Slope $\dfrac{-4 - 4}{3 - (-1)} = \dfrac{-8}{4} = -2$. Then $4 = -2(-1) + b$ gives $b = 2$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers 6 to 10</summary>

6. $y = -\tfrac{1}{3}x + 2$. The perpendicular slope is $-\tfrac{1}{3}$. Then $1 = -\tfrac{1}{3}(3) + b$ gives $b = 2$.
7. $(3, -1)$. Add the equations: $4x = 12$. Then $3(3) + 2y = 7$ gives $y = -1$. Check: $3 - 2(-1) = 5$.
8. 25 small and 15 large. $s + l = 40$ and $3s + 8l = 195$. Substitute $s = 40 - l$: $120 + 5l = 195$ and $l = 15$.
9. $3x^2 y^2$. The top is $27x^6 y^3$. Divide: $\tfrac{27}{9} = 3$, $x^{6-4} = x^2$, $y^{3-1} = y^2$.
10. $4 \times 10^5$. $8 \times 5 = 40$ and $10^{-3} \cdot 10^7 = 10^4$. Then $40 \times 10^4 = 4 \times 10^5$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers 11 to 15</summary>

11. $(x - 7)(x + 4)$. Then $x = 7$ or $x = -4$. The numbers $-7$ and 4 multiply to $-28$ and add to $-3$.
12. Discriminant $(-5)^2 - 4(2)(-3) = 25 + 24 = 49$. $x = \dfrac{5 \pm 7}{4}$ gives $x = 3$ or $x = -\tfrac{1}{2}$.
13. $x = 20$. Alternate interior angles are equal: $3x + 15 = 5x - 25$ gives $40 = 2x$. Both angles are $75^\circ$.
14. $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$: $6x = 180$ gives $x = 30$. A right triangle.
15. 10.5 m. The sun rays are parallel. The stick and pole triangles have matching angles and are similar (AA). $\dfrac{h}{14} = \dfrac{1.5}{2}$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers 16 to 20</summary>

16. Any correct proof counts. The rearrangement proof: a square of side $a + b$ holds four copies of the right triangle. Placed one way, they leave a tilted square of area $c^2$ uncovered. Placed another way, they leave two squares of area $a^2$ and $b^2$. Same big square minus the same four triangles: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$.
17. 10. $\Delta x = 8$, $\Delta y = -6$, and $\sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100}$.
18. $14\pi \approx 43.98$. $\pi r^2 = 49\pi$ gives $r = 7$.
19. $100 - 25\pi \approx 21.46$. The quarter circle is $\tfrac{1}{4}\pi(10^2) = 25\pi$.
20. $V = \pi(4^2)(9) = 144\pi$. $SA = 2\pi(16) + 2\pi(4)(9) = 32\pi + 72\pi = 104\pi$. Cone: $\tfrac{1}{3} \times 144\pi = 48\pi$.

</details>

## Ready for 301 If

Score each question 0 (wrong or blank), 1 (right idea, slip in the arithmetic or the steps), or 2 (right with every step shown). The maximum is 40.

| Area | Questions | Ready when you score |
|---|---|---|
| Linear equations and inequalities | 1 to 3 | 5 of 6 |
| Functions and lines | 4 to 6 | 5 of 6 |
| Systems | 7 to 8 | 3 of 4 |
| Exponents and quadratics | 9 to 12 | 6 of 8 |
| Angles and triangles | 13 to 17 | 8 of 10 |
| Area and volume | 18 to 20 | 4 of 6 |

You are ready for [Course 301](../301/README.md) if all of these hold:

* Total score of 32 or more out of 40.
* Every area meets its row in the table. A high total does not cover for a weak area. 301 leans on each of them.
* Questions 5, 11, 12, and 17 score 2. Lines, factoring, the quadratic formula, and the distance formula return constantly in 301, where trigonometry is built on right triangles and the unit circle.
* You can do question 16 without notes. Explaining why a formula holds is the habit 301 and every later level depends on.

If you fall short, redo the lessons named next to the missed questions, then retake only those areas a few days later.
Missing a checkpoint is information, not a verdict. Fix the area, rerun the check, ship.
