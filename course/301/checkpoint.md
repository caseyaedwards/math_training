# 301 Checkpoint

**Level:** 301 · **Time:** 90 to 120 minutes · **Back to:** [301 overview](README.md)

Twenty questions, one or two per lesson. Work on paper with a calculator, but no notes. Write every step. Then open the answers and mark each question right or wrong. A question with several parts counts as right only if every part is right.

The lesson for each question is in brackets. Review that lesson for any question you miss.

## Questions

1. [Lesson 1] Find the domain of $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{x + 1}}{x - 3}$ in interval notation.
2. [Lesson 1] Let $f(x) = x^2 - 1$ and $g(x) = 2x + 3$. Find $f(g(1))$ and $g^{-1}(x)$.
3. [Lesson 1] Describe $y = -(x + 2)^2 + 5$ as a transformation of $y = x^2$ and give the vertex.
4. [Lesson 2] For $p(x) = -x(x - 2)^2$, list the zeros with crossing or touching behavior, and describe the end behavior.
5. [Lesson 2] Compute $(2 + i)(3 - 2i)$. Then solve $x^2 - 2x + 5 = 0$.
6. [Lesson 3] For $r(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - x - 6}$, find the hole, the vertical asymptote and the horizontal asymptote.
7. [Lesson 4] A service has 2,000 users, growing 15% per month. How many users after 12 months? How long does it take to double?
8. [Lesson 5] Simplify $\log_2 40 - \log_2 5$. Then solve $4^x = 50$ to three decimal places.
9. [Lesson 5] How many bits do you need to give 100,000 objects distinct IDs? A p99 latency moves from 20 ms to 2 s. How many orders of magnitude is that?
10. [Lesson 6] A right triangle has hypotenuse 12 and an angle of $35°$. Find the side opposite that angle.
11. [Lesson 7] Give exact values of $\sin\frac{5\pi}{4}$, $\cos\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$ and $\tan\frac{2\pi}{3}$.
12. [Lesson 7] Convert $225°$ to radians, and find the arc length it cuts on a circle of radius 8.
13. [Lesson 8] For $y = 3\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}x\right) - 1$, give the amplitude, period, midline, maximum and minimum.
14. [Lesson 8] $\cos\theta = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is in quadrant IV. Find $\sin\theta$ and $\sin 2\theta$.
15. [Lesson 9] A triangle has sides 6 and 10 with a $120°$ angle between them. Find the third side and the area.
16. [Lesson 9] Given $A = 40°$, $a = 5$ and $b = 7$, how many triangles are possible? Give each possible angle $B$.
17. [Lesson 10] Find the center and radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 10y + 20 = 0$. Then find the foci of $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{20} = 1$.
18. [Lesson 11] Find $5 + 8 + 11 + \dots + 62$. Then find $12 - 4 + \frac{4}{3} - \dots$
19. [Lesson 12] Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{x^2 - 16}{x - 4}$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{5x^3 + x}{2x^3 - 7}$.
20. [Lesson 12] Write the secant slope of $y = x^3$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 1 + h$, simplify it, and find its limit as $h \to 0$.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $[-1, 3) \cup (3, \infty)$. The root needs $x \ge -1$. The denominator bans $x = 3$.
2. $g(1) = 5$, $f(5) = 24$. $g^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 3}{2}$.
3. Left 2, flipped over the x-axis, up 5. Vertex $(-2, 5)$.
4. Zero at 0 (multiplicity 1, crosses) and at 2 (multiplicity 2, touches). Degree 3 with leading coefficient $-1$: left end up, right end down.
5. $6 - 4i + 3i - 2i^2 = 8 - i$. Discriminant $4 - 20 = -16$, so $x = 1 \pm 2i$.
6. $\frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)}$. Hole at $x = 3$, height $\frac{6}{5}$: the point $\left(3, \frac{6}{5}\right)$. Vertical asymptote $x = -2$. Horizontal asymptote $y = 1$.
7. $2000 \cdot 1.15^{12} \approx 10,700$ users. Doubling time $\frac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.15} \approx 4.96$ months (rule of 70: about 4.7).
8. $\log_2 \frac{40}{5} = \log_2 8 = 3$. $x = \frac{\ln 50}{\ln 4} \approx 2.822$.
9. $\log_2 100,000 \approx 16.6$, so 17 bits. $\frac{2 \text{ s}}{20 \text{ ms}} = 100$: two orders of magnitude.
10. $12 \sin 35° \approx 6.88$.
11. $-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$; $\frac{1}{2}$; $-\sqrt{3}$.
12. $\frac{5\pi}{4}$. Arc length $8 \cdot \frac{5\pi}{4} = 10\pi \approx 31.42$.
13. Amplitude 3. Period $\frac{2\pi}{\pi/6} = 12$. Midline $y = -1$. Maximum 2. Minimum $-4$.
14. $\sin\theta = -\frac{4}{5}$ (quadrant IV, $y < 0$). $\sin 2\theta = 2\left(-\frac{4}{5}\right)\left(\frac{3}{5}\right) = -\frac{24}{25}$.
15. $c^2 = 36 + 100 - 120\cos 120° = 136 + 60 = 196$, so $c = 14$. Area $\frac{1}{2}(6)(10)\sin 120° = 15\sqrt{3} \approx 25.98$.
16. $\sin B = \frac{7 \sin 40°}{5} \approx 0.8999$. $B \approx 64.1°$ or $B \approx 115.9°$. Both fit ($40 + 115.9 < 180$): two triangles.
17. $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 4 + 25 - 20 = 9$: center $(2, -5)$, radius 3. Ellipse: $c^2 = 36 - 20 = 16$, foci $(\pm 4, 0)$.
18. $d = 3$, number of terms $\frac{62 - 5}{3} + 1 = 20$, sum $\frac{20(5 + 62)}{2} = 670$. Geometric with $r = -\frac{1}{3}$: $\frac{12}{1 + 1/3} = 9$.
19. Factor: $\frac{(x - 4)(x + 4)}{x - 4} = x + 4 \to 8$. Equal degrees: $\frac{5}{2}$.
20. $\frac{(1 + h)^3 - 1}{h} = \frac{3h + 3h^2 + h^3}{h} = 3 + 3h + h^2 \to 3$.

</details>

## Ready for 401 If

Count your right answers, then check the must-haves.

| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 18 to 20 | Ready. Start [401 Calculus](../401/README.md). |
| 15 to 17 | Nearly ready. Review the lessons behind each miss, redo those exercises, then start 401. |
| 11 to 14 | Not yet. Spend a week on the missed lessons, then retake the checkpoint. |
| 10 or fewer | Rework the level in order. There is no shame in a second pass; most people need one. |

Must-haves, regardless of score. Calculus leans on these every day, so all of them need to feel routine:

* Functions: you compose, invert and shift graphs without hesitation (questions 1 to 3).
* Algebra under pressure: you factor and cancel cleanly, including complex-root quadratics (questions 4 to 6).
* Exponentials and logs: you move between $b^y = x$ and $\log_b x = y$ and solve for an exponent (questions 7 and 8).
* Trig: you evaluate sine and cosine at the special angles in radians and use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ (questions 11 to 14).
* Limits: you can explain in plain words what $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ means, and you can compute a secant slope and let $h \to 0$ (questions 19 and 20).

One last test, without notes: draw the unit circle with its special angles, the graph of $2^x$ next to $\log_2 x$, and a parabola with its secant lines, and explain each picture out loud for one minute. If you can, you are ready.
