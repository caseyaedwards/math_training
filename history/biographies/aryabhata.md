# Aryabhata

**Lived:** 476 to 550 · **Where:** Kusumapura (near Patna), India · **Era:** [Chapter 04: India and China](../04-india-and-china/README.md)

Aryabhata is the Indian astronomer and mathematician who, at 23, writes a compact verse textbook with a sharp value of pi, early sine tables, and the claim that the Earth spins.

The birth year comes from his own text: he says he is 23 years old 3,600 years into the current age, which works out to 499. The death year of 550 is a later estimate. The timeline marks 499 twice: the year of the *Aryabhatiya* and of his pi value, 62,832 / 20,000.

## The World Around Them

Kusumapura, the old capital Pataliputra, sits in the Gupta Empire, a period of strong science and literature. Astronomers serve religion and daily life: festival dates, eclipse predictions, and calendars depend on them. Texts take the form of short verses, built for memorizing, not for explaining.

## What They Did

* Writes the *Aryabhatiya* in 499: 121 verses on arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and astronomy.
* Gives pi as about 3.1416 and calls the value "approaching", a hint that he knows it is not exact.
* Builds a table of **sine differences**: how the half-chord of a circle grows as the angle grows. This is the root of trigonometry.
* Solves equations of the form $ax - by = c$ in whole numbers with a method called **kuttaka**, "the pulverizer".
* States that the Earth rotates on its axis and explains eclipses by shadows, not by demons.

## Key Idea, Worked Small

**His pi verse.** In words, the *Aryabhatiya* says: add 4 to 100, multiply by 8, and add 62,000. The result is about the circumference of a circle whose diameter is 20,000.

```txt
  100 + 4 = 104                    add four to one hundred
  104 x 8 = 832                    multiply by eight
  832 + 62,000 = 62,832            add sixty-two thousand
  circumference = 62,832           for a diameter of 20,000
  pi = 62,832 / 20,000             pi is circumference over diameter
     = 3.1416                      true value: 3.14159...
```

The large diameter is a trick to avoid fractions: whole numbers carry four correct decimal places.

## A Human Detail

India names its first satellite *Aryabhata* and launches it in 1975. Fourteen centuries after he insists the Earth turns, his name goes up and watches it turn.
