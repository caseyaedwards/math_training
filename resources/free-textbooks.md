# Free Textbooks

Complete math books that are free to read online or download, grouped by course level. Every link was fetched and checked in October 2026. Each one opens the book itself or the author page that hosts it.

For short pages and lecture notes, see the main [resource list](README.md). To recheck these links, run `go run ./resources/linkcheck/main.go` from the repository root.

Tags: `[html]` reads in a browser, `[pdf]` downloads as PDF, `[exercises]` includes problems, `[answers]` includes solutions, `[classic]` is a public-domain book written before 1930.

## How to Pick a Book

* For a first look at a topic, start with the friendliest book in the level (marked **gentle**).
* For practice, pick a book tagged `[exercises]` and `[answers]`.
* Classic public-domain books on Project Gutenberg use old notation and old language. They are slow but rewarding. Use them as a second read, not a first.

* [Amusements in Mathematics](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16713), Henry Ernest Dudeney (1917): hundreds of arithmetic and logic puzzles with solutions. `[html]` `[classic]` `[answers]`

## 501 Linear Algebra, Probability and Discrete Math

### Linear Algebra

* [Linear Algebra](https://hefferon.net/linearalgebra/), Jim Hefferon (Saint Michael's College): a full course with a complete answer book. **gentle** `[pdf]` `[exercises]` `[answers]`
* [A First Course in Linear Algebra](https://linear.pugetsound.edu/), Robert Beezer (University of Puget Sound): an open text with many worked examples. `[html]` `[exercises]` `[answers]`
* [Introduction to Applied Linear Algebra: Vectors, Matrices, and Least Squares](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/vmls/), Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe (Stanford): linear algebra for data fitting and machine learning. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
* [Kenneth Kuttler Books](https://klkuttler.com/), Kenneth Kuttler: free texts on elementary linear algebra, differential equations, and analysis. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`

* [Notes on Discrete Mathematics](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/aspnes/classes/202/notes.pdf), James Aspnes (Yale): long, friendly course notes from a computer science view. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
* [Combinatorics and Graph Theory](https://www.whitman.edu/mathematics/cgt_online/book/), David Guichard (Whitman College): counting, recurrences, and graphs. **gentle** `[html]` `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
* [Foundations of Combinatorics with Applications](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~ebender/CombText/), Edward Bender and S. Gill Williamson (UC San Diego): a full counting text in PDF chapters. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
* [generatingfunctionology](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/DownldGF.html), Herbert Wilf (University of Pennsylvania): the classic short book on generating functions. `[pdf]` `[exercises]` `[answers]`
* [A=B](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/AeqB.html), Marko Petkovšek, Herbert Wilf, and Doron Zeilberger: computer proofs of sum identities, with a foreword by Donald Knuth. `[pdf]`

### Graph Theory

* [Random Walks and Electric Networks](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~doyle/docs/walks/walks.pdf), Peter G. Doyle and J. Laurie Snell (Dartmouth): random walks on graphs pictured as circuits. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
* [Introduction to Random Graphs](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/BOOK.pdf), Alan Frieze and Michał Karoński (Carnegie Mellon): a graduate text on random graph models. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
* [Spectral Graph Theory](https://fanchung.ucsd.edu/research/revised.html), Fan Chung (UC San Diego): chapters on eigenvalues of graphs. `[pdf]`

### Probability and Statistics

* [OpenIntro Statistics](https://www.openintro.org/book/os/), David Diez, Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel, and Christopher Barr: a widely used intro text. `[pdf]` `[exercises]` `[answers]`
* [Introductory Statistics 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/introductory-statistics-2e), Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean (OpenStax): a standard first course. `[html]` `[pdf]` `[exercises]` `[answers]`

### Information Theory

* [Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms](https://www.inference.org.uk/itila/book.html), David J. C. MacKay (Cambridge): entropy, compression, and error-correcting codes with many pictures. `[pdf]` `[exercises]` `[answers]`
* [Foundations of Data Science](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/jeh/book.pdf), Avrim Blum, John Hopcroft, and Ravindran Kannan: high-dimensional geometry, random walks, and algorithms for data. `[pdf]` `[exercises]`
